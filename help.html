Getting started

Lumen Notes is a note app for studying. You write notes as plain text, and Lumen turns parts of them into things you can use: quizzes, flashcards, diagrams, charts and sliders. Your notes stay on your PC. There is no account.

Open Lumen and make a note

  1. Open Lumen from the Start menu. It opens on Home.
  2. Press Ctrl+N, or click New note in the sidebar. A menu offers a blank note or a template (see Writing and organizing notes).
  3. Type a title at the top and start writing.

Lumen saves as you type. The saved indicator in the top bar shows when everything is written to disk.

Try an example first

Home shows a welcome card the first time you open Lumen. It has three ways to start:

  • Paste from an AI chat. Opens Paste as visual notes.
  • Explore the Thyroid page. A finished study page with a concept map, a simulator and a quiz. Click around in it.
  • Start from a template. Lecture notes, homework, a study guide, reading notes or a project log.

The welcome card goes away when you dismiss it or once you have written your own notes.

The three views

Every note can be shown three ways. Switch with Ctrl+Shift+M, or with the buttons at the top of the note.

ViewWhat you seeUse it to
VisualThe finished page. Quizzes, charts and diagrams work.Read, study and make small edits.
SourceThe plain text the page is made from.Write exact text and blocks.
SplitSource on one side, the page on the other.Watch a block appear as you type it.

Switching views never changes your text. If Lumen does not understand a piece of markup, it leaves the text alone and shows it as source with a short reason.

Find anything

Press Ctrl+K. Type part of a title, a word you remember, a tag like #biology, or a concept. Use the arrow keys to move, Tab to filter by kind, and Enter to open.

Where help is

The ? button in the top bar opens this Help. Ctrl+F in Settings jumps to the settings search.

Writing and organizing notes

Kinds, folders, tags and stars

Each note has a kind: Note, Page or Task. The kind decides which sidebar place lists it. It does not change what you can put in it.

  • Folders (Collections). Set a note's collection in its properties. Use a slash for a folder inside a folder: Science/Biology. Right-click a folder and choose Customize… to give it a color and an icon.
  • Tags. Add tags in the note's properties, or write #tag in search to find them. Turn on Pick tags automatically in Settings › Editor › Tags and Lumen suggests tags for new notes.
  • Favorites. Click the star at the top of a note.
  • Properties. Click + Properties under the title to set the kind, collection and tags.
  • Icon and cover. Click the icon next to the title to pick an emoji. + Customize page opens the icon, cover, font, width and music for that one page.

Typing in a note

A note is a list of blocks. A paragraph is a block, a heading is a block, a quiz is a block.

  • Press Enter at the end of a paragraph to start a new text block.
  • Double-click a text block to edit it. Bold is Ctrl+B, italic is Ctrl+I, underline is Ctrl+U.
  • Start a line with #, ## or ### for headings. Start with - for a bullet list, 1. for a numbered list and - [ ] for checkboxes.
  • Put math between dollar signs: $x^2$, or between $$ lines for its own line.
  • Code goes between three backticks, with the language after the opening ones.
  • Tables use the Markdown form: | A | B | with a | --- | --- | line under the first row.

Press / while you are not typing in a field to add a block from a list (see Adding and arranging blocks).

Links between notes

Write the title of another note between double square brackets:

You writeYou get
[[Negative feedback]]A link to that note.
[[Thyroid|the thyroid note]]A link that shows your own words.
[[Thyroid#Feedback]]A link to the "Feedback" heading inside that note.

A link to a note that does not exist yet is dashed. Click it and Lumen creates the note.

Under every note Lumen shows:

  • Linked mentions. Notes that link here, with the sentence around the link.
  • Related notes. Notes that share tags, links or a folder, with the reason.
  • Not written yet. Links in this note that point to notes that do not exist, with a button to create each.

Graph in the sidebar draws every linked note. Turn on Tags to draw tags as dots too, or Notes without links to show the rest. Search in the graph by title, concept or #tag.

Templates

New note offers templates:

  • Blank
  • Lecture notes (titled with today's date)
  • Homework set (dated)
  • Study guide
  • Reading notes
  • Project log

To make your own, open a note you like, click ⋯ and choose Save as template. It then shows under Your templates.

Version history and deleting

⋯ › Version history lists earlier versions of the note. Open one to see it, then restore it.

⋯ › Duplicate note makes a copy. ⋯ › Delete note removes the note and its review history. Before you delete many notes, make a backup (see Backups and sync).

Quick capture

Press Ctrl+Alt+Space from any app to open a small box. Type a thought, pick a folder and press Enter. It goes into today's quick note or a new note. Esc cancels. It works while Lumen is minimized, and after you close the window if Keep running in the tray is on (it is by default). Change the shortcut or turn the tray off in Settings › Editor › Opening and capture. To quit Lumen completely, use the tray icon's menu.

Adding and arranging blocks

Add a block

Two ways:

  1. Press / while you are not typing in a text box. A list opens. Type to filter it, then press Enter.
  2. Type the block's text yourself in Source or Split view.

The whole list is on the Every block page. The first four entries (Drawing, Picture, 3D model, 3D settings) open a picker. The rest add a working example you then change.

Change a block

  • Text blocks: double-click and type.
  • Other blocks: point at the block and click Edit, or open its ⋯ menu and choose Edit block. The editor opens beside the note, and the block stays on screen as a live preview.

In the editor:

  • The outline on top lists the block's parts. Click a part to edit it. The arrow keys move through the outline.
  • Fields write straight into the block. Only the thing you changed is rewritten. Your spacing and order stay as you typed them.
  • A value that would break the block is refused with the reason. The note keeps its last good version.
  • Charts edit their data as a table. Quizzes mark the right answer with a round button.
  • Appearance sets alignment, text size, font, accent color, background strength, corner rounding and spacing for that one block.
  • Source shows the block's own text.
  • Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z undo and redo.

Move blocks

  • Drag a block by its ⋮⋮ handle. Drop it on the top or bottom half of another block to put it above or below.
  • Alt+↑ and Alt+↓ move the block you picked. The block's ⋯ menu has move buttons too.
  • While you drag near the top or bottom edge, the page scrolls.

Columns

Drop a block near the left or right edge of another block and the two sit side by side. A row holds up to four columns, and each column can hold several blocks. Columns stack when each would be narrower than 200 px.

Take out of columns in a block's ⋯ menu puts it back below the row.

In the source, columns are ordinary text between comments, so other Markdown apps just show the blocks one after another:

<!-- columns -->

## Hypothyroid

Too little hormone.

<!-- column -->

## Hyperthyroid

Too much hormone.

<!-- end columns -->

Select, copy and paste blocks

  • Triple-click a block to select it. Ctrl+click or Shift+click adds more.
  • Ctrl+C copies the selected blocks. Ctrl+X cuts them. Ctrl+V pastes them at the bottom of the note. Right-click where you want them to go and choose paste to put them there.
  • The copy is also plain Markdown, so you can paste it into another app.

The rules for block text

Blocks are written as small tags. You rarely need to write them yourself, but when you do:

  • Put a block at the start of a line, with a blank line before and after it.
  • Close every tag. <card title="Idea">…</card>, or <flashcard … /> for a block with no content.
  • Put attribute values in quotes.
  • Give every interactive block an id. Lumen uses it to remember your answers. If you leave it out, Lumen adds one the first time you use the block.
  • Inside a block, text is Markdown. If it has < or & in it, wrap it in <![CDATA[ … ]]>.

Lumen forgives two common slips. A stray & or < in text (like R&D or < 5%) is read as the character itself. A block with a missing closing tag ends at the next blank line. Both still show up correctly, but write them properly when you can.

Anything Lumen does not know stays in the note as visible source, with a short note about what is wrong. Nothing is deleted.

Every block

Add any of these from the / menu (see Adding and arranging blocks). Each one below shows what it is for and the text Lumen puts in the note when you add it. You can change that text in Source or Split view, or with the block's visual editor.

Interactive blocks keep your answers, sliders and ticks in the note itself, so they come back the next time you open it. Blocks with an id keep that state when you move them, so leave the id alone.

Text and structure

Basic pieces that every note uses.

Heading

A section title. Headings fill the page outline and let you jump with links like [[Note#Heading]].

## A new thought

Text

A normal paragraph. Double-click it to edit. Bold, italic, links and lists work as in any Markdown app.

Start writing here.

Task list

Checkboxes. Ticking one saves right away. Unticked items also show up in the To-dos card on Home, with the note they came from. Double-click a task's words to edit the list as text.

- [ ] First task
- [ ] Next task

Styles. Open the block's ⋯ menu and pick a Task list style:

  • Plain: the list as it is.
  • Numbered steps: a number beside each box, for things done in order.
  • Cards: each task in its own tile.
  • Compact: tight rows and smaller text, for long lists.
  • Progress header: "2 of 5" and a bar above the list that fills as you tick.
  • Two columns: a long list flows into two columns (one on a narrow page).

The same menu sets Align (left, centre, right) and Width (narrow, medium, full). A styled list is written as a <checklist> around the same tasks, and its tasks still show on Home and the Tasks page. Picking Plain, Left and Full turns it back into a plain list.

<checklist id="checklist-1" title="Meeting prep" variant="progress" align="center" width="medium">
- [ ] General check in
- [ ] Bring up linear algebra
</checklist>

Equation

A formula typeset with LaTeX. Put it between $$ lines for its own line, or between single $ signs inside a sentence.

$$
E = mc^2
$$

Callout

A tinted box that makes one idea stand out. The word in brackets sets the kind: info, tip, warning, danger, question, example or quote.

> [!info] Worth remembering
> Add a useful insight here.

Card

A framed box with a title. Put one focused idea in it. Cards can sit side by side in a grid.

<card title="A useful idea">
Write your **Markdown** here.
</card>

Divider

A line, dots or space between sections. Add label to put a word in the middle.

<divider />

Expandable section

Hides a worked answer or a long detail until you click it. It remembers whether it is open.

<reveal id="reveal-1" label="Show the worked example">
  <text>Write the hidden explanation here.</text>
</reveal>

Two columns

Two cards side by side. Change columns from 1 to 4. The columns stack when the window is narrow. You can also drag one block onto another block's side to make columns (see Adding and arranging blocks).

<grid columns="2">
  <card title="One perspective">Add your notes.</card>
  <card title="Another perspective">Add your notes.</card>
</grid>

Questions and practice

Blocks you answer. Flashcards, practice questions and missed multiple choice questions also go into Review (see Flashcards and review).

Flashcard

A card that turns over. The front is the question, the back is the answer. It is added to Review.

<flashcard id="flashcard-1" front="A question to remember" back="The answer is here." />

Practice question

A question with a hidden answer. Click to reveal it. It is added to Review.

<exercise id="exercise-1" question="What did you learn?" answer="Write your answer here." />

Multiple choice

One question with options. answer="0" means the first option is correct (counting starts at 0). Wrong answers are added to Review.

<question id="quiz-1" prompt="Choose the correct answer." answer="0" explanation="Explain why here.">
  <option>Correct answer</option>
  <option>Another possibility</option>
</question>

Knowledge check with feedback

Several questions in one block, with a score, a progress bar and a reset. layout="paged" shows one question at a time. Add hint to give a nudge.

<quiz id="quiz-1" title="Check your understanding" tag="Practice" layout="paged">
  <question id="quiz-1-1" prompt="Choose the correct answer." answer="0" explanation="Explain why this is right." hint="An optional nudge.">
    <option>Correct answer</option>
    <option>Another possibility</option>
  </question>
  <question id="quiz-1-2" prompt="A second question." answer="1" explanation="Explain the answer.">
    <option>Not this one</option>
    <option>This one</option>
  </question>
</quiz>

Fill in the blanks

A sentence with blanks. Write each answer between {{ and }}. Use | between answers that are also correct. You type, press Check, and can press Show answers.

<cloze id="cloze-1" title="Fill in the blanks">The {{mitochondrion}} makes most of the cell's {{ATP|energy}}.</cloze>

Matching pairs

Two to twelve pairs. The right side is shuffled and you tap or drag to match, then see a score.

  <pair left="Osmosis" right="Water moving toward more solute" />
  <pair left="Diffusion" right="Particles spreading out" />
  <pair left="Active transport" right="Moving against a gradient using energy" />
</matching>

Two to twelve steps. Write them in the correct order. Lumen shuffles them and you put them back in order.

Contrast table

Two things people mix up, side by side. Each trait is a row. tell is one line for telling them apart.

Numbers, charts and models

Blocks where you change a number and watch the result change.

Stat tiles

Big numbers with an arrow for up, down or flat. Put them in a grid.

  <stat label="Another measure" value="Low" trend="down" tone="info" />

Chart

A line, bar, area, donut or radial chart. type picks the kind. points is the data. You can edit the data as a table in the visual editor.

Flashcards and review

Lumen turns the flashcards and questions in your notes into a daily review. It decides when each card comes back using FSRS-6, the same method Anki uses. It works offline and needs no AI.

Which blocks become cards

BlockFront of the cardBack of the card
Flashcardits frontits back
Practice questionits questionits answer
Multiple choicethe question and optionsthe right option and the explanation

Flashcards and practice questions are always cards. A multiple choice question becomes a card after you get it wrong, in the note or while studying. You can turn this off in Review › Settings › Review questions I get wrong.

A card's topic is the heading above it, or its quiz's title.

Make a flashcard from text you selected

  1. Select text in a note.
  2. Choose Make flashcard.
  3. A draft opens with your selection as the back. Lumen fills in the front when the text already looks like a term and its meaning, like Term: meaning, X is … or a short first line above more text. Otherwise you write the front.
  4. Save it. The card is added below the block you selected from.

Do a review

  1. Click Review in the sidebar. A number beside it is how many cards are due.
  2. The page shows cards due today, new cards for today, reviews done today, your streak and a 14-day forecast.
  3. Start a session. Think of the answer, then press Show answer (Space).
  4. Grade yourself:
GradeKeyUse it whenWhat happens
Again1You did not remember.Comes back in about 1 minute.
Hard2You remembered with effort.Comes back sooner than Good.
Good3You remembered.Comes back later, in days after the first few times.
Easy4It was effortless.Comes back much later.

Each grade button shows when it would bring the card back. A multiple choice question you answer wrong is graded Again for you.

At the end Lumen counts your grades and offers Retry the ones you missed.

Choose what to study

  • Review studies today's due cards, for everything, a folder, a tag or one note.
  • Practice all goes through every card in that group, in random order.
  • Study this note in a note's toolbar practices only that note, and returns you to it when you finish.

New cards per day is 20 by default. Change it in Review › Settings (0 to 200).

Hints

Any card can have a hint, a nudge that does not give the answer away. It stays hidden until you press Show hint (H). Write one in the block's Hint field. If you saved a Gemini key (Settings › Smart features), a card without a hint shows Suggest a hint, and Gemini writes one and saves it on the card. Each suggestion uses one of your day's Gemini requests.

Where your progress is kept

Review history is saved inside the note the card is in. Backups and exports of your workspace include it. Deleting a note deletes its review history. A card is remembered by its id, so you can reword it without losing its schedule.

Semester grades

Backups and sync

Where your notes live

Lumen keeps your notes in a database on your PC, in your Windows user folder. Settings › Sync & backup › Your data shows the exact path. Nothing is uploaded unless you set up one of the features below.

Backups

Lumen makes backups on its own, and you can make one whenever you want.

  • Daily snapshots. Each time Lumen starts, it saves a copy of the database next to it, at most once every 20 hours. The seven newest are kept.
  • Back up now (Ctrl+Shift+E). Saves your whole workspace as one file you choose a place for. It holds your notes, answers, review history and settings.
  • Restore points. Before Lumen does anything big (restoring a backup, moving pictures), it keeps a restore point of your notes.

Restore from a backup

  1. Open Settings › Sync & backup › Restore from a backup.
  2. The list shows daily backups, restore points and any backup file you choose. Each has a date and a note count. Press Refresh if a new one is missing.
  3. Press Preview to compare it with your notes. Each note is marked missing, different or matching.
  4. Restore everything, or tick single notes.

Lumen keeps a restore point of your current notes first, so a restore can be undone.

Import

Ctrl+O imports a Lumen backup or a Markdown file. Imports add notes. A note with an id you already have gets a new id, so both copies are kept. Your current look and settings stay as they are.

Back up to Google Drive

This uses Google Drive for desktop. Lumen only writes into a folder on your PC, and Google Drive uploads it.

  1. Install Google Drive for desktop and sign in.
  2. In Lumen open Settings › Sync & backup › Google Drive and Skybridge.
  3. Under Lumen folder, choose a folder inside Google Drive, like G:\My Drive\Lumen Inbox.
  4. Turn on Daily backup to this folder. Once a day Lumen saves a full backup in a Backups folder (the last 7) and a readable copy of each note.
  5. Press Back up now to test it.

Drive can also copy big files (pictures, 3D models, PDFs, videos). There is a row for each in the same tab.

Keep two PCs in sync

If you use Lumen on two PCs, you can keep them the same. Notes, folders, to-dos, events and study progress all match.

  1. Set a Lumen folder in Google Drive on both PCs (above).
  2. On each PC open Settings › Sync & backup › Keep your PCs in sync.
  3. Turn on Sync through Google Drive. It shows how many other PCs it found.
  4. Optional: turn on Also link directly on the same Wi-Fi. It is faster and encrypted, and falls back to Drive if the link drops.

Lumen checks every 10 seconds while you are not typing. Press Sync now to check right away. Changes from Drive show up after Google Drive uploads them.

If you edit the same note on two PCs before they sync, Lumen keeps both. One is saved as a copy ending in "(sync conflict)". A badge appears on the Sync tab. Open Sync conflicts to compare them.

Sync appearance is off by default and each PC chooses for itself. When on, your theme, fonts and layout match on every PC. API keys, your local AI address, window size and volumes never sync.

Windows Firewall

The direct link needs Lumen allowed on private networks. Windows asks the first time. A guest Wi-Fi often blocks PC-to-PC traffic. If direct linking fails, use Drive sync.

Export and share

Export a note as a PDF or a web page

  1. Open the note.
  2. Click the download icon in the note toolbar (Export note), then PDF or web page…. You can also use ⋯ › PDF or web page….
  3. Choose:
OptionChoices
FormatPDF or Web page
ColorsYour current theme, or Light, for printing
Paper (PDF)Letter, A4, Legal, A5, or Long page (one page as tall as the note, nothing split)
Include answersQuiz answers, flashcard backs and practice answers. Leave off for a blank worksheet.
Include videos (web page)Puts the video files inside the page. It makes the file bigger.
Add a sign-offYour name, the date and your logo at the end
  1. Press Export PDF or Export web page.

The web page

A web page export is one .html file. Pictures, fonts and videos are inside it. It opens in any browser, works offline, and you can email it or put it on a website. Quizzes, flashcards, sliders, tabs and the computer architecture blocks work in the page. Diagrams, charts, 3D models and PDFs show as still pictures. Answers you give in the exported page are not saved. The page has a real phone layout.

The PDF

Diagrams, charts and simulations are drawn as still pictures, with every tab and hidden section opened. A note's background picture is not part of a PDF.

Sign-off

A sign-off puts your name, the date and an optional logo at the end of an export. Set it up in Settings › You › Sign-off, or tick Add a sign-off in the export window. To use your own logo, press Add your logo and choose a picture. Lumen ships without any logo, so a logo only shows if you add one.

Markdown and backups

  • ⋯ › Markdown file saves the note's exact source as a .md file.
  • Ctrl+Shift+E saves your whole workspace.
  • Import Markdown files at the bottom of the sidebar adds .md files as notes.

Draft a note from a PDF

⋯ › Import › Draft a note from a PDF… reads a PDF and starts a note from it.

Privacy

The full policy is in Settings › Privacy policy, at the bottom of the side list. The short version:

  • Lumen has no account and no sign-in.
  • Your notes, pictures, files, to-dos, events, flashcard history and settings are stored only on your PC.
  • Lumen has no analytics, no crash reports, no advertising and no tracking.
  • Lumen connects to the internet only for features you turn on, and only to the service that feature needs.

Your data is not encrypted on disk. Anyone who can sign in to your Windows account can read it. Use a Windows password and lock your PC. Files in Google Drive follow Google's rules.

Delete everything

Uninstall Lumen, then delete its data folder. Settings › Sync & backup › Your data shows where it is. Export a backup first if you want to keep your notes.

Troubleshooting

A note or a block looks wrong

  • Switch to Source or Split view. Blocks Lumen cannot show are left as source with a reason under them.
  • Every block needs a closing tag (</card>) or a /> at the end. Attribute values need quotes.
  • A < or & inside a block's text needs to be written &lt; and &amp;, or wrapped in <![CDATA[ … ]]>.
  • Quiz answers are counted from 0. answer="0" is the first option.

I lost a note or changed it by mistake

  1. Open the note and choose ⋯ › Version history.
  2. If the note is gone, open Settings › Sync & backup › Restore from a backup. Look in the daily backups and restore points. Preview, then restore just that note.

The saved indicator does not say saved

Wait a moment and look again. If it keeps showing a problem, Lumen could not write to disk. Check that your disk has space, then make a backup with Ctrl+Shift+E.

Menus or scrolling feel slow

  • Turn off Settings › Appearance › Motion › Enable background animation.
  • Choose the Clear picture look or a plain color for the background.
  • Turn off the visual layer in Settings › Home › Weather and effects.
  • If you have older pictures inside notes, use Settings › Sync & backup › Pictures › Move pictures. Notes then save faster.

Google Drive backup does nothing

  • Check that Lumen folder points to a folder inside Google Drive and that Google Drive for desktop is running.
  • Press Back up now and read the message under it.
  • Look in the Backups folder in that folder.

PCs do not sync

  • Both PCs need the same Lumen folder in Google Drive and Sync through Google Drive on.
  • Google Drive must finish uploading before the other PC can see the change.
  • For the direct link, allow Lumen through Windows Firewall on private networks. Guest Wi-Fi often blocks it.
  • Press Sync now and read the status line.

Gemini does not answer

  • Check the Gemini API key in Settings › Smart features.
  • Lumen allows up to 15 Gemini requests a day. It tells you when you reach it.
  • Check your internet connection.

Local AI does not connect

  • Start the AI app first (LM Studio or Ollama) and load a model.
  • Check the Local AI address. LM Studio's is usually http://localhost:1234/v1.
  • Press Test local AI, then Refresh local AI models.

Voice or read aloud does not work

The first time, Lumen downloads a voice model, so it needs internet once. Check your microphone is allowed in Windows privacy settings.

No sound

Sound effects are off by default. Turn them on in Settings › Sounds › Interface sounds. Nothing plays while Lumen is hidden or another window is in front.

Still stuck

Open Settings and press Report a problem at the bottom of the side list. It opens an email draft with your Lumen version. Describe what happened and what you expected. The draft never includes your notes.
